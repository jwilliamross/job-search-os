# Outreach

## Principles

- One specific reason for the message, stated in the first two lines.
- Ask for one small thing with a time box (15 minutes, a call this week).
- Never claim what is not true. "I applied on Tuesday" beats "I am very interested."
- One follow-up 5-7 days later, one more a week after that, then stop.
- Short. Sixty to ninety words for busy people. Slightly more formal for senior people,
  never stiff.

## Who to find

Priority: (1) alumni of the user's school at the firm, (2) alumni of their high school or
hometown, (3) anyone sharing an activity (a sport, a club, a competition), (4) anyone the
user has met through past jobs or their own business, (5) analysts and associates one to
three years out of school (they answer; senior people mostly do not), (6) campus recruiters
named on the posting. Use the user's `profile/network/` export if present. Record everyone
in `applications/<folder>/notes.md` and `outreach.md`.

## Message set (draft all in the user's voice; see `profile/VOICE.md`)

1. **Cold email / LinkedIn note (60-120 words).** Who they are in one line, the specific
   reason for writing (the person's path, a deal, a post), the ask with a time box.
2. **Follow-up (40-70 words).** Reference the first note, add one new thing (an application
   submitted, a link), repeat the ask once.
3. **Pre-call prep (in `notes.md`).** Five questions specific to the person; two things the
   user can offer.
4. **Thank-you (50-90 words, same day).** One specific thing they said, one action the user
   will take because of it, and the door left open.
5. **Referral ask (only after a real conversation).** Direct: "Would you be comfortable
   flagging my application to the recruiter?"

## Output

Write drafts to `applications/<folder>/outreach.md` with a row per person. Return the
drafts in code blocks. The user sends them; Claude never sends on their behalf without an
explicit instruction and a final confirmation of recipient and text.
